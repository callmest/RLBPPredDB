Help

Common questions and quick answers for using RLBPPredDB.

1. What is RLBPPredDB?

RLBPPredDB is an integrated database portal for R-loop binding protein (RLBP) prediction resources. On this website, you can access proteome-level prediction records, confidence scores, functional annotations, species-filtered browsing, and downloadable database files for local analysis.

2. Which species are currently included in RLBPPredDB?

The current database includes whole-proteome RLBP prediction data for Human (Homo sapiens) and Mouse (Mus musculus).

3. What does the confidence score mean?

The confidence score is the model-predicted probability (0 to 1) that a protein is an RLBP. A higher score indicates a higher likelihood of R-loop binding.

4. What confidence threshold is recommended for high-confidence candidates?

A threshold of 0.85 is recommended as the primary cutoff. It provides a practical balance between controlling false positives and retaining a useful number of candidates.

5. Why can I not find some specific UniProt proteins in the database?

During data cleaning, proteins shorter than 50 amino acids and proteins without usable 3D structure files in PDB or AlphaFold were excluded.

6. What is the source of the functional annotation field?

Functional annotations are primarily integrated from UniProt protein naming and gene annotation fields, with supporting evidence cross-referenced from GO, InterPro, and Pfam domain resources.

7. What is the core technical framework of the RLBPPred model?

The model uses a two-stage architecture with an upstream encoder and downstream classifier: SaProt-650M-AF2 extracts sequence-structure features, and a balanced-sampling soft-voting ensemble MLP performs final classification.

8. How accurate is the RLBPPred model?

The best model achieved an AUROC of 0.9925 and an AUPRC of 0.9264 on an independent validation set, outperforming traditional random forest methods based on hand-crafted features.

9. Have high-scoring candidates been experimentally validated?

Yes. High-scoring candidates such as LIG4 and IGHMBP2 were validated by S9.6 antibody immunoprecipitation with RNase H sensitivity assays, supporting their physical association with R-loops.

10. Can I download raw data, and do I need a backend server to use this database?

Yes. Data files are available on the Data Access page in JSON and CSV formats for local analysis. No backend service is required for browsing and filtering; RLBPPredDB uses a lightweight static-page architecture served from the user's own computer.
